

# 2. The Fundamental Profit Equation

For every rig, every day, CESTOS should be able to calculate:

**Rig Contribution = Revenue generated − Direct operating cost**

And:

**Revenue**

= Billable metres × rate/metre

* mobilisation/demobilisation
* standby charges
* additional services
* equipment/support charges

**Direct Cost**

= Labour

* fuel
* drilling consumables
* bits/rods/tools
* spare parts
* maintenance
* vehicles/logistics
* camp/site costs
* subcontractors
* allocated project overhead

This leads to perhaps the most important KPI in the entire company:

### Contribution margin per rig

Management should know something like:

> TD900-01
> Project: XYZ Gold
> Revenue this month: $84,200
> Direct cost: $59,600
> Contribution: $24,600
> Contribution margin: 29.2%

And compare that against every other rig.

Right now, a rig could be extremely busy while making surprisingly little money.

That is precisely what the Geodrill numbers demonstrate at larger scale: record revenue did not prevent margin compression. ([Newswire][2])

Your equipment module should therefore go substantially beyond storing equipment records.

# 5. Control the Biggest Profit Leak: Downtime

A drill rig that is parked because a $300 component wasn't available can cost far more than the component itself.

COS therefore needs a proper maintenance engine:

**Rig → System → Component → Maintenance schedule → Inspection → Fault → Work order → Parts → Technician → Repair → Cost → Downtime**

Importantly, failure history should become searchable.

For example:

> TD900-01
> Hydraulic pump failure
> 17.5 hours downtime
> Parts: $2,180
> Labour: $380
> Lost estimated drilling contribution: $4,700
> Total economic impact: $7,260

Now management sees the **economic cost of maintenance failures**, not merely the repair bill.

Eventually AI can identify:

> "Rig 3 has experienced three similar hydraulic-pressure events within 60 days. Inspect pump assembly before next shift."

That's genuinely useful AI.

# 6. Inventory Should Be Connected to Production

I would not operate inventory as an isolated warehouse application.

Every item should ultimately connect back to:

**Purchase → Warehouse → Issue → Employee → Rig → Project → Drill hole → Cost**

Then management can determine:

**Consumables cost/metre by rig**
**Consumables cost/metre by project**
**Fuel cost/metre**
**Parts cost/metre**
**Emergency purchase frequency**
**Inventory shrinkage**
**Supplier performance**

The system should also automatically calculate minimum/reorder stock for mission-critical components.

A spare that can stop a $500k+ asset from operating should not be managed using the same rules as office stationery.

# 7. Procurement Can Become a Major Margin Lever

Every purchase should follow:

**Request → Approval → RFQ → Supplier comparison → PO → Delivery → Inspection → Warehouse → Invoice → Payment**

The system should record historical pricing.

If something previously cost $850 and a supplier quotes $1,300, procurement should see the variance immediately.

More importantly, purchasing should eventually be forecast from scheduled work.

If CESTOS signs a 12,000 m diamond program, COS should estimate required:

* drilling consumables
* oils/lubricants
* common replacement components
* fuel
* PPE
* staffing
* vehicles/support requirements

That enables bulk procurement rather than expensive emergency buying.

# 8. Completely Redesign the Sales Engine

This may produce even more profit than the internal platform.

CESTOS shouldn't wait for someone to announce a tender.

Create a permanent **Mining Intelligence + Business Development Engine**.

Liberia currently provides a good environment for doing this. ArcelorMittal is expanding production toward 20 Mtpa; Zodiac has been conducting an aggressive multi-target drilling program; the government is trying to advance Putu; and the National Investment Commission describes Liberia as substantially underexplored beyond its traditional commodities. ([ArcelorMittal][3])

COS should maintain an account/opportunity record for every serious exploration and mining company.

Then track events such as:

**Exploration licence granted → financing raised → geophysical survey → drilling announced → tender → resource estimate → feasibility → expansion**

For example, a junior explorer raising millions of dollars and announcing a 10,000–20,000 m program is a **commercial signal**.

CESTOS should know about it immediately.
